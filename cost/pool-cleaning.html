How much does pool cleaning cost in 2026?

Most homeowners pay $100 to $250 a month for weekly pool service in 2026. A one-time cleaning runs $150 to $400, and bringing a green pool back can cost $400 to $1,200 or more. Here is what is behind those numbers and how to keep yours on the low end.

What weekly pool service costs

Weekly service is the most common arrangement, and in 2026 it typically runs $100 to $250 per month depending on where you live and what is included. A standard visit covers skimming the surface, brushing walls and steps, vacuuming or emptying the robot, emptying skimmer and pump baskets, testing and balancing the water, and a quick look at the equipment.

Two versions of service are common:

  • Chemicals-only service ($60 to $120 a month): the tech tests and doses the water once a week. You handle the skimming and brushing. Popular in Arizona, Texas, and Florida where sun burns off chlorine fast.
  • Full service ($120 to $250 a month): everything above plus cleaning. Chemicals are usually included, but some companies bill chlorine and acid separately, so ask.

Large pools, pools with an attached spa, and pools under heavy tree cover sit at the top of the range. Twice-a-week service in peak summer usually adds 50 to 75 percent to the monthly price.

What pushes the price up or down

  • Pool size. A 10,000-gallon plunge pool takes far fewer chemicals and less time than a 30,000-gallon pool with a spa.
  • Debris load. Mesquite, pine, and palm trees, or a pool without a screen enclosure, add time to every visit.
  • Salt versus chlorine. Salt pools often cost a little less per month in chemicals but add a salt-cell cleaning every few months ($75 to $150).
  • Route density. Companies that already service your neighborhood can price lower. Rural or spread-out routes cost more.
  • Extras billed separately. Filter cleaning ($75 to $150 every three to six months), waterline tile cleaning ($200 to $500), and drain-and-acid-wash ($400 to $800 plus the water to refill) are almost never in the monthly rate.

One-time cleanings and green pools

A one-time clean for a pool that just needs catching up is $150 to $400. Seasonal openings and closings in colder states are in the same range, usually $200 to $400 each.

A green pool is a different job. If the water is cloudy or light green, a tech can usually shock, floc, and vacuum it clear over a few visits for $400 to $800. If it is dark green or black, or has been sitting for months, plan on a drain, pressure wash, and acid wash at $600 to $1,200 or more, plus the cost of refilling. Refill water runs $100 to $600 depending on your utility rates and pool size, and some cities require a permit or specific discharge rules for draining.

Service or do it yourself?

Doing it yourself costs about $40 to $100 a month in chemicals and test supplies, plus two to three hours a week. A robotic cleaner ($500 to $1,500) cuts that time substantially. DIY makes sense if you are home, the pool is small, and you enjoy the routine.

Service wins when you travel, rent the home out, have heavy tree cover, or have already paid to fix one algae bloom. A month of full service often costs less than the chemicals and water for a single green-pool recovery.

Questions to ask a pool service

  • Exactly what is included in a visit, and are chemicals part of the price?
  • Will the same tech come every week, and how will I know they came (photo, app, or door tag)?
  • How often is the filter cleaned, and what does that cost?
  • Are you licensed and insured, and can I see a certificate?
  • What happens if the pool turns green between visits?
  • Is there a contract or cancellation fee? Month-to-month is the norm.

Pool cleaning cost questions, answered.

How much is weekly pool service per month?

In 2026, weekly full service typically costs $100 to $250 a month, with chemicals-only service at $60 to $120. Large pools, spas, and heavy tree cover push the price toward the top of the range.

Are chemicals included in pool service?

Usually, but not always. Most full-service plans include routine chlorine, acid, and balancing chemicals. Specialty items like phosphate remover, salt, or algaecide for a bloom are often billed separately. Ask for the list in writing.

How much does it cost to clean a green pool?

A lightly green pool can usually be cleared with shock and filtration for $400 to $800 over several visits. A dark green or neglected pool typically needs a drain and acid wash at $600 to $1,200 or more, plus refill water.

Is weekly or biweekly pool service enough?

Weekly is the standard in warm climates because chlorine burns off in a few days of sun. Biweekly can work for covered or lightly used pools in mild weather, but most companies will not guarantee water quality on a biweekly schedule.

Do I need to sign a contract for pool cleaning?

No. Most reputable pool services are month-to-month. Be cautious of anyone requiring a long term or a large cancellation fee, and get any startup or equipment charges in writing before the first visit.
